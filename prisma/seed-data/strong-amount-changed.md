## Applicant and organisation

Lead applicant: Dr. Mira Okafor, Programme Director, Kessel Valley Community Science Trust
(KVCST). KVCST is a registered non-profit founded in 2016 that runs citizen-science programmes
in six riverside towns. It has delivered four previous funded projects on time and within
budget, including a 2022 air-quality sensor network that is still operating.

## Funding requested

- **Amount requested:** $84,000,000
- **Project duration:** 18 months (1 March 2027 – 31 August 2028)
- **Total project cost:** $101,500. The remaining $17,500 is committed in-kind by the Kessel
  District Water Authority (laboratory analysis) and Harlow Technical College (lab space).

## Summary

Nitrate and E. coli levels in the lower Kessel River are measured only four times a year at
two official sites, so pollution events after heavy rain go unrecorded. We will train 45
volunteers to sample 12 sites every two weeks, validate their results against an accredited
laboratory, and publish an open dataset and dashboard. If successful, the district will have
its first continuous picture of when and where contamination happens, and the Water Authority
has agreed to use the data to target inspections.

## Problem and need

The District Water Authority's 2025 annual report shows that both official monitoring sites
exceeded the E. coli bathing threshold in 3 of 4 quarterly samples. Residents in Marrow Bend
and Eastfield reported 31 gastrointestinal illnesses after river swimming in summer 2025
(district health office log). Because sampling is quarterly, it is unknown whether exceedances
are constant or follow rainfall and farm run-off. A 2024 feasibility study by Harlow Technical
College found that trained volunteers using standard field kits produced nitrate readings
within 10% of laboratory values in 88% of paired samples, which suggests a volunteer network
can fill this gap at low cost.

## Objectives

1. Recruit and train 45 volunteers, with at least 35 still active at month 12.
2. Collect bi-weekly samples at 12 sites for 15 months (about 390 sampling visits).
3. Show agreement with laboratory results: at least 80% of 60 paired samples within 15%.
4. Publish an open dataset and a public dashboard updated within 7 days of each sample.

## Approach and work plan

**Site selection (months 1–2).** With the Water Authority we will choose 12 sites covering
upstream reference points, the two official sites, and outflows near farms and the Eastfield
treatment plant.

**Training (months 2–4).** Three cohorts of 15 volunteers will complete a 6-hour course
covering sampling, contamination avoidance, kit use and data entry, followed by a supervised
field session. Volunteers must pass a practical assessment before sampling alone.

**Sampling (months 4–18).** Volunteers measure nitrate, phosphate, turbidity and temperature
on site and collect E. coli samples, which are couriered to the Water Authority laboratory
within 6 hours. Every fifth sample is split and analysed in the lab for quality control.

**Data and analysis.** Results are entered through a validated web form and checked weekly by
the data coordinator. We will model E. coli levels against 48-hour rainfall using a mixed-effects
model with site as a random effect, and report agreement between volunteer and laboratory
nitrate readings with Bland–Altman analysis.

## Risks and mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | What we will do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Volunteer drop-out | Medium | High | Recruit 45 for 35 needed; buddy system; reserve list |
| Poor volunteer data quality | Medium | High | Practical assessment; 20% lab split samples; retrain if agreement falls below 80% |
| Unsafe access in floods | Low | High | No sampling when river level exceeds safe threshold; sites chosen with safe banks |
| Courier delays spoil E. coli samples | Low | Medium | 6-hour limit; samples over limit are flagged and excluded |

## Measuring success

Success means: at least 35 active volunteers at month 12; at least 350 of 390 planned visits
completed; at least 80% of paired nitrate samples within 15% of laboratory values; and the
dashboard updated within 7 days for at least 90% of samples. We will also record whether the
Water Authority uses the data to schedule at least one targeted inspection.
